Abstract

This study examines how the 2023–24 boycott movement affected customer retention at KFC Pakistan. Drawing on a survey of 105 respondents and branch-level sales data from KFC Lucky One Mall, Karachi, it assesses switching intention, boycott behaviour, and the perceived quality and attractiveness of local alternatives.

Results show that 74.3% of respondents are inclined to switch to local brands, that daily branch sales fell by roughly 47%, and that Kababjees is most often viewed as the leading alternative. The study concludes that cultural relevance, brand ethics and community trust are decisive for retention in Muslim-majority markets, and recommends menu localisation, improved service and transparent communication.

Chapter 01

Introduction &
Background

120+Outlets nationwide
Oct 2023Boycott begins

KFC entered Pakistan in 1997 with its first outlet in Gulshan Iqbal, Karachi, and has since grown to more than 120 outlets nationwide, becoming one of the country’s most recognised international quick-service brands.

In October 2023, public reaction to the Israel–Palestine conflict triggered a global consumer boycott movement. In Pakistan, KFC, alongside other multinational brands, became a focal point of that sentiment as consumers linked its parent company, Yum! Brands, to alleged Israeli ties. The campaign spread through social media, street protests and, in some cases, physical attacks on outlets, and it coincided with a measurable fall in sales.

Market Position

More than 120 outlets since 1997 gave KFC wide national reach, so any shift in consumer sentiment carried real commercial weight.

The Trigger

From October 2023, boycott campaigns circulated lists of “Israel-linked” brands, with KFC and McDonald’s among the most visible targets.

Mobilisation

Hashtags such as #BoycottKFC and #BoycottPSL spread across Twitter, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, turning online sentiment into purchasing behaviour.

Problem Statement

Despite KFC’s long presence and strong brand recognition in Pakistan, the boycott raised a question for management: do attitudinal reactions such as guilt, ethical objection and a preference for local brands translate into lasting customer switching, and how much revenue is at stake? This study addresses that question by combining consumer perceptions from a survey with branch-level sales data.

Research Objectives
  1. Measure switching intention among KFC customers during the boycott.
  2. Quantify the sales impact at KFC Lucky One Mall, Karachi, and relate it to the industry-wide decline.
  3. Assess the perceived quality and attractiveness of local alternatives such as Kababjees.
  4. Recommend retention strategies for KFC Pakistan.
Research Questions
  1. RQ1. To what extent do consumers intend to switch from KFC to local brands?
  2. RQ2. How large was the commercial decline during the boycott period?
  3. RQ3. Which alternatives are consumers turning to, and why?
Significance & Scope

The findings are relevant to brand and operations managers of multinational franchises exposed to sentiment-driven demand shocks. The scope is limited to Pakistani consumers (n = 105) and sales data from a single branch, so results should be read as indicative rather than nationally representative.

Chapter 06

Conclusion &
Recommendations

The boycott had a profound and measurable impact on KFC's customer retention in Pakistan. Over 74% expressed willingness to switch to local brands, while KFC outlets experienced a ~47% decline in daily sales. The movement permanently reshaped the Pakistani fast-food landscape.
